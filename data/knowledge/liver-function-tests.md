# Liver Function Tests

## What Are Liver Function Tests?

Liver function tests (LFTs, also called a liver panel) examine blood samples to measure substances produced by the liver. Common measurements:

- **Albumin** — a protein made by the liver
- **Total protein** — all proteins in blood, primarily liver-made
- **Liver enzymes:** ALP (alkaline phosphatase), ALT (alanine transaminase), AST (aspartate aminotransferase), GGT (gamma-glutamyl transferase)
- **Bilirubin** — waste product from broken-down red blood cells
- **LDH** (lactate dehydrogenase) — enzyme found throughout body tissues
- **PT** (prothrombin time) — measures clotting time; prothrombin is liver-made

These tests reveal liver function and damage but typically require additional testing for diagnosis.

## Why Tests Are Ordered

To identify liver disease symptoms, assess disease severity, monitor conditions over time, and check medication side effects. Included in routine comprehensive metabolic panels (CMP) or ordered for symptoms like jaundice, fatigue, nausea, or abdominal pain.

## What Abnormal Results Mean

Abnormal patterns can indicate hepatitis, liver inflammation, compromised liver function, bile duct problems, or medication-related damage. Other conditions can cause similar abnormalities, so providers consider overall clinical context. Common patterns:
- **Hepatocellular** (AST/ALT predominantly elevated): viral hepatitis, fatty liver, alcohol, drug-induced injury.
- **Cholestatic** (ALP/GGT/bilirubin predominantly elevated): bile duct obstruction, primary biliary cholangitis.
