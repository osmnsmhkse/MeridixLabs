# Type 2 Diabetes

## Definition

Type 2 diabetes is characterized by elevated blood glucose levels. The body either fails to produce sufficient insulin or cannot utilize it effectively, preventing glucose from entering cells properly to provide energy. Over time, excess blood glucose can cause serious health complications, though management strategies can help prevent or delay these outcomes.

## Causes and Risk Factors

Type 2 diabetes typically develops through a combination of being overweight, physical inactivity, and genetic predisposition. The condition usually begins with insulin resistance — cells don't respond normally to insulin. Initially, the body compensates by producing more insulin, but eventually cannot maintain adequate production, and blood glucose rises.

Higher-risk individuals: age over 45, prediabetes, family history, excess weight, Black/Hispanic/Latino/American Indian/Asian American/Pacific Islander ethnicity, high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, polycystic ovary syndrome, depression, low HDL cholesterol, elevated triglycerides, acanthosis nigricans.

## Symptoms

Often absent initially. When present: increased thirst and urination, heightened hunger, fatigue, blurred vision, numbness or tingling in extremities, slow-healing sores, unexplained weight loss.

## Diagnosis

- **A1C test** — measures average blood glucose over the past 3 months. ≥6.5% = diabetes.
- **Fasting plasma glucose** — 8+ hours fasted. ≥126 mg/dL = diabetes.
- **Random plasma glucose** — ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms = diabetes.

## Treatment

Maintain healthy blood glucose through lifestyle (diet, regular physical activity, glucose monitoring) and, when needed, medications. Maintain target blood pressure and cholesterol.

## Prevention

Weight loss, reduced calorie intake, and increased physical activity can prevent or delay type 2 diabetes onset.
