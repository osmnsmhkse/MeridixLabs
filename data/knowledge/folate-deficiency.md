# Folate-Deficiency Anemia

## Overview

Folate-deficiency anemia occurs when the body does not have enough healthy red blood cells due to insufficient folate (vitamin B9, also called folic acid). The body has limited folate storage, so consistent dietary intake is necessary.

## Causes

- Inadequate dietary folate
- Long-term alcoholism
- Medications interfering with folate metabolism: phenytoin (Dilantin), **methotrexate**, sulfasalazine, triamterene, pyrimethamine, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, barbiturates
- Poor nutrition (especially in elderly populations, or with overcooked food lacking fresh vegetables)
- Pregnancy (heightened folic acid demands)
- Hemolytic anemia
- Weight-loss dieting

This is a megaloblastic anemia: red blood cells are abnormally large (macrocytes/megaloblasts in bone marrow).

## Symptoms

Fatigue, weakness, headache, pallor, sore mouth and tongue.

## Diagnosis

Typically a CBC plus red blood cell folate level. Bone marrow aspiration in rare cases.

## Treatment

Identify and address the underlying cause. Folic acid supplementation orally, intramuscularly, or IV. Dietary modifications: more green leafy vegetables and citrus fruits. Patients with intestinal malabsorption may need lifelong treatment.

## Prognosis

Folate-deficiency anemia most often responds well within 3 to 6 months, particularly when underlying causes are addressed.
