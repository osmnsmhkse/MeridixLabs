# C-Reactive Protein (CRP) Test

## What CRP Measures

A CRP test measures inflammation in the blood. The liver produces CRP in response to inflammation — the body's protective response to injury, infection, or disease. Acute inflammation is helpful and temporary; chronic inflammation can damage healthy tissues and often arises from autoimmune disorders or repeated irritation from smoking or environmental chemicals.

## Why CRP Tests Are Ordered

To assess inflammation and help diagnose various conditions. Indications include symptoms of bacterial infection (fever, chills, rapid heart rate, rapid breathing); monitoring chronic inflammatory conditions; post-surgical infection screening; tracking response to treatment for infections or inflammatory disorders such as sepsis.

## What High Results Indicate

Elevated CRP may signal acute or chronic conditions:
- Bacterial or viral infections
- Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's, ulcerative colitis)
- Autoimmune disorders (lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, vasculitis)
- Lung disease such as asthma

Smoking and environmental toxin exposure can also elevate levels.

## Normal Ranges

A healthy CRP level is 0.8–1.0 mg/dL or lower. CRP may also rise with insomnia, depression, hormone replacement therapy, obesity, or sex differences (females typically slightly higher than males).

## Standard CRP vs. High-Sensitivity CRP (hs-CRP)

Standard CRP measures general inflammation. **hs-CRP** detects very small increases and is specifically designed to estimate cardiovascular disease risk.
