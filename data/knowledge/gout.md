# Gout

## Definition

Gout is a common type of inflammatory arthritis that causes pain, swelling, and redness in one or more joints. Flares typically persist for 1–2 weeks before resolving. The big toe and lower limbs are common sites of initial presentation.

## Causes

Gout develops when uric acid accumulates excessively. Mechanism: purine metabolism — when cells break down or purines from food are digested, uric acid forms as a byproduct. Normally, kidneys filter this waste. When the body produces excess uric acid or fails to eliminate enough, serum levels rise — **hyperuricemia**. In susceptible individuals, uric acid crystallizes into needle-like structures within joints, triggering symptoms.

## Risk Factors

Male sex, older age, obesity. Comorbidities: heart failure, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, chronic kidney disease. Conditions accelerating cellular breakdown (psoriasis, certain cancers). Family history. High-purine diets (red meat, organ meats, certain seafood). Alcohol. Fructose-rich foods. Medications: **diuretics**, low-dose aspirin, immunosuppressants. High-dose niacin.

## Symptoms

Acute flares often start suddenly at night with intense pain — sometimes severe enough to wake the patient — plus swelling, redness, and warmth. Extended untreated gout produces **tophi**: hard uric acid deposits beneath the skin, progressing from painless to painful, potentially causing bone and tissue damage.

## Diagnosis

History and physical. Synovial fluid analysis under microscopy for uric acid crystals (gold standard). Serum or urine uric acid testing. Ultrasound or CT to detect crystal accumulation and exclude alternatives.

## Treatment

Acute flare: NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen), colchicine, or corticosteroids. Prevention: weight loss, alcohol limitation, purine-restricted diet. Urate-lowering pharmacotherapy (e.g., allopurinol, febuxostat) when indicated. With early intervention, gout is one of the most controllable forms of arthritis.
