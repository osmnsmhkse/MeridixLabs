# Creatinine Test

## What the Test Measures

Creatinine is a waste product generated when muscles break down during normal activity. Healthy kidneys filter creatinine from blood and eliminate it via urine. Abnormal levels may indicate kidney dysfunction.

## Why It Is Ordered

To assess kidney function, diagnose kidney disease, monitor existing kidney conditions, and check medication side effects. Commonly part of routine checkups — particularly for patients with diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, or a family history of kidney problems. Also ordered for symptoms like swelling, fatigue, urination changes, or difficulty concentrating.

## Understanding Results

### High Blood Creatinine

May indicate kidney disease or injury, infection, poor renal blood flow, urinary obstruction, kidney failure, or conditions like heart failure or diabetes. Non-renal causes also elevate creatinine: dehydration, muscle disorders, intense exercise, high meat consumption, pregnancy complications.

### Low Blood Creatinine

May signal malnutrition, muscle-wasting conditions from prolonged illness, nerve disorders, age-related muscle loss, or serious liver disease. Relatively uncommon.

## Important Considerations

Creatinine alone is incomplete. Results are typically used to calculate:
- **eGFR** (estimated glomerular filtration rate)
- **Creatinine clearance**
- **Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR)**

Individual factors — muscle mass, diet, age, activity — influence results, so normal ranges vary per person. Reference ranges differ between males and females.
