# Anemia

## Definition

Anemia occurs when blood lacks sufficient oxygen-carrying capacity. The condition typically stems from insufficient iron, which the body requires to produce hemoglobin — the protein responsible for blood's red color and oxygen transport.

## Primary Causes

Three main mechanisms: blood loss, inadequate red blood cell production, or accelerated destruction of existing red cells. Common triggers: heavy menstrual bleeding, pregnancy complications, GI ulcers, cancerous polyps. Dietary insufficiency of iron, folic acid, or vitamin B12 frequently contributes. Inherited blood disorders (sickle cell disease, thalassemia) also cause anemia.

## Symptoms

Fatigue, cold sensitivity, dizziness, mood changes, shortness of breath, headaches. Symptom severity depends on the type and rate of onset.

## Diagnosis

Physical exam plus blood testing. Core tests:
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)** — measures hemoglobin and hematocrit.
- Iron panel (iron, ferritin, TIBC).
- Hemoglobin electrophoresis when hereditary conditions are suspected.

## Treatment

Depends on the specific type identified. Interventions include iron supplementation, vitamin replacement therapy (B12, folate), or treating the underlying cause.
