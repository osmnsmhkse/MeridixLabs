# Hypokalemia (Low Blood Potassium)

## Overview

Hypokalemia occurs when potassium levels in the blood fall below normal. Potassium is essential for cell function and is regulated by the kidneys. Normal range: 3.5–5.1 mEq/L (mmol/L).

## Causes

- **Medications:** diuretics (water pills), certain antibiotics like amphotericin B
- **GI losses:** diarrhea, vomiting, laxative overuse
- **Eating disorders:** bulimia
- **Endocrine:** hyperaldosteronism, Cushing syndrome
- **Kidney disease:** chronic kidney disease impairs regulation
- **Mineral deficiencies:** low magnesium
- **Fluid loss:** excessive sweating
- **Genetic disorders:** hypokalemic periodic paralysis, Bartter syndrome

## Symptoms

Mild–moderate: constipation, palpitations or skipped beats, fatigue, muscle weakness or spasms, tingling.

Severe: abnormal heart rhythms, lightheadedness, fainting, potentially fatal cardiac arrest — especially with pre-existing heart disease.

## Diagnosis

Serum potassium plus glucose, magnesium, calcium, sodium, phosphorus, thyroid function, aldosterone. ECG to assess cardiac effects.

## Treatment

Mild: oral potassium supplements. Severe: IV administration. For patients requiring diuretics, switch to potassium-sparing formulations or add supplemental potassium. Diet: bananas, spinach, salmon, avocados, beans, tomatoes.

## Complications

Untreated severe hypokalemia can cause life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias and, rarely, paralysis.
