# Calcium

## What Is Calcium and Why It Is Needed

Calcium is the most abundant mineral in the human body. It builds and maintains strong bones and teeth, enables muscle movement, facilitates nerve communication between the brain and body, supports blood vessel function, and releases hormones that regulate body processes.

## Dietary Sources

Dairy (milk, cheese, yogurt) — main sources for most Americans. Leafy greens: kale, broccoli, bok choy. Fish with edible soft bones: canned sardines and salmon. Calcium-fortified foods: breakfast cereals, fruit juices, soy/rice beverages.

## Daily Requirements

- Infants: 200–260 mg
- Children 1–8: 700–1,000 mg
- Children/teens 9–18: 1,300 mg
- Adults 19–50: 1,000 mg
- Women 51+ and all adults 71+: 1,200 mg

## Supplementation

- **Calcium carbonate** — absorbed best when taken with food.
- **Calcium citrate** — absorbed effectively regardless of meal timing.
- Optimal absorption at doses ≤500 mg at once.
- Side effects (gas, bloating) may be managed by spreading doses or switching forms.

## Insufficient Calcium

Can lead to osteoporosis, rickets in children, and osteomalacia (soft bones). Higher risk: children 4–18, Black and Asian individuals, older adults in poverty, postmenopausal women, non-dairy consumers.

## Blood Calcium Abnormalities

- **Hypercalcemia** (high blood calcium) — most commonly caused by primary hyperparathyroidism or malignancy; other causes include vitamin D excess, immobilization, sarcoidosis. Symptoms: fatigue, confusion, constipation, polyuria, kidney stones.
- **Hypocalcemia** (low blood calcium) — causes include hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, chronic kidney disease, magnesium imbalance. Symptoms: muscle cramps, perioral tingling, tetany, seizures.
