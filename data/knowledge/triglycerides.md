# Triglycerides

## What Are Triglycerides?

Triglycerides are a type of fat — the most prevalent fat form in the body. They come from dietary sources (butter, oils, fatty foods) and from surplus calories the body stores for later energy. When fuel is needed, the body mobilizes stored triglycerides. VLDL cholesterol particles transport triglycerides through the bloodstream.

Elevated triglyceride levels increase the risk of heart disease, including coronary artery disease.

## Causes of High Triglycerides

- Consuming more calories than expended, especially sugar
- Overweight or obesity
- Cigarette smoking
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Certain medications
- Genetic disorders
- Thyroid disease
- Poorly managed type 2 diabetes
- Liver or kidney disease

## Triglyceride Level Guidelines (mg/dL)

- **Normal:** less than 150
- **Borderline High:** 150–199
- **High:** 200–499
- **Very High:** 500 and above

Levels exceeding 150 mg/dL elevate heart disease risk and are a metabolic syndrome risk factor.

## Treatment

Lifestyle modifications: weight management, regular physical activity, smoking cessation, reducing sugar and refined foods, limiting alcohol, replacing saturated fats with healthier alternatives. For some individuals, cholesterol medications may be needed to further reduce triglycerides.
