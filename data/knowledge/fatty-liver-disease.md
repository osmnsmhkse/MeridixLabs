# Fatty Liver Disease

## What is Fatty Liver Disease?

Fatty liver disease occurs when fat accumulates excessively in liver tissue. Two primary forms: nonalcoholic and alcohol-related.

## Types

**Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD)** — develops independently of heavy alcohol use. Two subtypes:
- **Simple fatty liver** — fat deposits without significant inflammation or cell injury; typically stable.
- **Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)** — inflammation and liver cell damage alongside fat. Inflammation can cause fibrosis (scarring).

**Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease** — results from excessive alcohol use. Severity increases with consumption.

## Risk Factors and Prevalence

NAFLD affects ~25% of the global population and is the most common chronic liver disorder in the United States. Elevated risk:
- Type 2 diabetes or prediabetes
- Obesity
- High blood lipids (cholesterol, triglycerides)
- Hypertension
- Certain medications (corticosteroids, some cancer treatments)
- Metabolic disorders
- Rapid weight loss
- Hepatitis C
- Hispanic or non-Hispanic White ethnicity

Middle-aged and older adults face higher risk, though children may develop the condition.

## Symptoms and Diagnosis

Both forms typically progress silently. When symptoms appear: fatigue or right upper quadrant abdominal discomfort.

Diagnosis often occurs incidentally through abnormal liver function tests. Workup: medical history (esp. alcohol), physical exam, blood tests, imaging. Liver biopsy in some cases to confirm and stage damage.

## Treatment

NAFLD: weight reduction reduces hepatic fat, inflammation, and fibrosis progression. If medications contribute, discontinue under medical supervision. No FDA-approved medications specifically treat NAFLD; research continues into diabetes medications and vitamin E.

Alcohol-related: stop drinking. Professional support and pharmacotherapy available.

## Complications

Both can progress to cirrhosis, potentially requiring transplantation if liver failure develops.

Lifestyle: heart-healthy nutrition, vaccinations (hepatitis A/B, flu, pneumococcal), regular physical activity, consult a clinician before supplements.
