# Prediabetes

## Definition

Prediabetes is an intermediate state where blood glucose levels are higher than normal but not high enough to be called diabetes.

## Causes

Two primary mechanisms: insulin resistance (the body cannot effectively use its own insulin) and insufficient insulin production. Major contributing factors are being overweight and sedentary lifestyles.

## Risk Factors

About one in three American adults has prediabetes. Higher risk: overweight, age 45 and older, family history of diabetes, certain racial/ethnic minorities, physical inactivity, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, history of gestational diabetes, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, or polycystic ovary syndrome.

## Symptoms

Most people experience no symptoms. Some may develop darkened skin patches in armpits or neck, sometimes with small skin growths in those areas (acanthosis nigricans).

## Diagnosis

- **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** 100–125 mg/dL = prediabetes.
- **A1C test:** 5.7%–6.4% = prediabetes.

## Treatment and Prevention

Lifestyle is the primary approach: weight loss for overweight individuals, regular physical activity, healthy eating plan with reduced calories. Diabetes medications may be recommended in select cases.

## Complications

Prediabetes increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and stroke.
