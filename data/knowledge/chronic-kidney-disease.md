# Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

## Definition

Chronic kidney disease is a condition in which the kidneys become damaged and cannot adequately filter blood. Waste accumulates and additional health complications can develop.

## How Kidneys Function

Each kidney (about fist-sized) filters blood to remove waste and excess water, producing urine. Kidneys also maintain chemical balance, regulate blood pressure, and produce hormones.

## Causes and Risk Factors

Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes. Kidney damage occurs slowly over many years, so many people have no symptoms until the disease is advanced.

## Diagnosis

Blood and urine tests are the only way to know if you have kidney disease. Key tests:
- **Serum creatinine** + **eGFR** (estimated glomerular filtration rate)
- **Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR)**

CKD stages (G1–G5) are defined by eGFR; albuminuria stages (A1–A3) by uACR.

## Treatment and Management

Treatments cannot cure CKD but may slow progression: medications to lower blood pressure (ACE inhibitors, ARBs), control blood sugar, and reduce cholesterol. Lifestyle: reduce sodium, heart-healthy diet, control BP and blood sugar, limit alcohol, weight loss if needed, regular physical activity, avoid smoking.

## Complications

CKD may progress to kidney failure, requiring dialysis or kidney transplantation. Cardiovascular disease risk is markedly elevated.
