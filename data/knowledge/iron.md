# Iron

## What Is Iron?

Iron is an essential mineral for growth and development. The body uses iron to produce hemoglobin — the protein in red blood cells that transports oxygen. Iron also supports healthy muscle function, bone marrow production, organ operation, and hormone synthesis.

## Dietary Sources

Rich sources: lean meats, seafood, poultry. Plant sources: beans, lentils, spinach, peas. Nuts and dried fruits (e.g., raisins) contribute too. Iron supplements are available independently or combined with multivitamins.

## Iron Deficiency: Causes and Risk Factors

While most Americans consume adequate iron, certain groups face greater deficiency risk:
- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding
- Premature or low-birth-weight infants
- Regular blood donors
- Cancer or digestive disease
- Kidney dialysis patients
- Celiac disease, ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, H. pylori infection (impair absorption)

## Symptoms and Health Consequences

Insufficient iron can progress to iron-deficiency anemia. Initial stages may be silent. Prolonged deficiency causes fatigue, shortness of breath, and trouble with memory and concentration. Iron supplements typically treat this condition.

## Iron Overload

Excessive iron accumulation damages tissues. High supplement intake can cause constipation, nausea and vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea. Extremely elevated levels risk ulcers, organ damage, coma, and death. Hemochromatosis is an inherited condition causing pathological iron buildup; treated with regular phlebotomy.
